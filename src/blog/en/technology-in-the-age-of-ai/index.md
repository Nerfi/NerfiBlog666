## What is happening now with AI?

### My honest opinion

It's not that I'm the person with the most experience in the tech sector, far from it. Sometimes I think, and I say this completely seriously, what the hell am I doing here? I've been programming in professional environments for about **4** years, it has been a road full of bumps: you work, and sooo much desire to quit, sometimes complicated companies, bad organization, projects nobody understands. Anyway, if any programmer reads me, they know what I mean and understand my point of view.

Now then, since the arrival of ***AI*** everything seems like chaos, and it really is. I think that what we have been seeing in the last two or three years is something that very few of us could have seen years ago, when code was written by ***hand***, how old that sounds, now with agents and models this intelligent practically nobody writes code by hand the way it used to be done, we delegate everything to an agent and wait to see the results, which, in my opinion, is neither good nor bad, as long as the results are something we control, at the very least we should understand the **specs** the agent creates for us. Most people believe or think that ***AI*** will take away jobs, which is not wrong, but the truth is that as time goes by I think that yes, it will take away jobs but I don't think all of us programmers will end up unemployed, I think it's more a matter of adapting, in this profession since I can remember that is what we have had to do, in one way or another long before the arrival of ***AI*** we were already living in a more automated world, I have worked in factories, I know what I'm talking about and even so I never lacked work in any of them.

What I'm trying to say is that I don't think we're going to run out of work, what I do think is that, as programmers, we have to drop our ego, and mine too, towards the agents and stop thinking that we are better than them, no my friend no, you are not better than **Opus**, and you have probably never met anyone better. We have to embrace this disruptive change, we have to, above all, learn to control it because an **agent** without control is like giving a child a gun, things will only go wrong, but if on the contrary we give it the necessary tools and we **control it**, with tests, with verifications, etc. I think we have something tremendously powerful that we can take advantage of, especially in consultancies, where they shuffle you between projects every couple of months and you have to learn a new technology every 6 months, which is not good for your mental health.

As a final note, just think about factories, I have worked in them and I have seen how the machine did practically everything, the human only did less specific tasks, but even so, whenever I wanted, I had work, but without a doubt, the ones who were never going to be left without work in a factory were and are the operators who know how to improve or do their job more efficiently with or thanks to a machine, or how to repair it.

As someone very intelligent once said: 

> It is not the strongest of the species that survives, nor the most intelligent that survives. It is the one that is most adaptable to change.
>
